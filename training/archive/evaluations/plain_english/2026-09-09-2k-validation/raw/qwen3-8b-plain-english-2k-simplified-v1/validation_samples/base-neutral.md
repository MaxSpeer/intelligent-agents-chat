I'm trying to study more effectively. What's active recall?

**assistant**

Active recall is a study technique where you actively retrieve information from memory instead of passively reviewing it. Instead of just reading through your notes or textbook, you test yourself by trying to remember the material without looking at the source. This strengthens memory retention and helps identify gaps in your understanding.

Examples of active recall include:

- Quizzing yourself
- Writing down key points from memory
- Using flashcards and trying to recall the answers
- Teaching the material to someone else
- Using apps like Anki or Quizlet

Active recall is more effective than passive review for long-term learning and is a key strategy in the "spaced repetition" method used in many effective study systems.

**user**

That sounds helpful. How does it compare to just re-reading my notes?

**assistant**

Active recall is much more effective than re-reading your notes, and here's why:

### **Re-reading Notes:**
- **What it is:** You read through your notes again and again.
- **What it does:** It reinforces the material to some extent, but it's **passive**. Your brain isn't actively trying to remember the information.
- **Limitation:** It doesn't challenge your memory or help you identify gaps in your understanding. You might think you know the material, but you may not actually remember it when you need to.

### **Active Recall:**
- **What it is:** You **actively retrieve** information from memory, often through self-testing or practice.
- **What it does:** It strengthens your memory by forcing your brain to work to recall the information. This process is **proven to improve retention** and understanding.
- **Advantage:** It helps you identify what you **don’t** know, so you can focus on those areas. It also mimics real-world learning, where you have to remember and apply information.

### **In Short:**
- **Re-reading** is like running on a treadmill — you're moving, but you're not really improving your fitness.
- **Active recall** is like lifting weights — it builds real, usable strength (in your memory).

For the best results, combine active recall with **spaced repetition** (reviewing material at increasing intervals) to maximize long-term retention.
